Ambitious Mechinterp and Activation Geometry — reading collection

Readings to accompany Eric Michaud and Brianna Grado-White’s lectures. Pick what interests you; this is more than a day’s reading. These are loosely grouped starting points, not a prescribed sequence. Importance scores prioritise this day’s reading: 5 marks the four anchor readings; 4 a recommended next choice; 3 useful depth; 2 an optional extension; 1 a specialist rabbit hole. Length is scored separately. You are not expected to read every high-rated item.

Start with Charles’s “What if not Circuits?”, section I. The rest of that essay is optional. As you read the papers below, ask what each takes a feature or circuit to be, what evidence would establish its existence, and what its methods actually measure.

Framing: what are we trying to explain?

What if not Circuits?, Charles Renshaw-Whitman, 2026 Importance ●●●●● Length ●●○○○

First reading: section I, “What Might We Mean By Circuits?” Distinguish assumptions about circuits from assumptions about representations; treat the essay as a perspective to examine, not a conclusion required by this notebook.

A Pragmatic Vision for Interpretability, Neel Nanda et al., 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

The explicit turn toward task-grounded, empirically evaluated interpretability; compare its success criteria with the ambition of reverse-engineering a model.

Dmitry’s Koan, Dmitry Vaintrob, 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●○○○

An explanation needs a specified precision scale: what error matters, relative to which baseline? Carry this question into the surrogate-model and causal-testing readings.

Representations and Toy Models of Superposition

Toy Models of Superposition, Elhage et al., 2022 Importance ●●●●● Length ●●●●○

The synthetic setting where features are known: investigate when sparse features share dimensions, how interference is filtered, and which conclusions depend on the toy setup.

Distributed Representations: Composition & Superposition, Chris Olah, 2023 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●○○○

Distinguishes compositional codes, superposition, and mere changes of basis. This is the likely intended “representation types” note, and is also linked from Charles’s opening essay.

Exercises: interference and what a probe proves

In the tied-weight toy model, x\in\mathbb R^n, W\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}, m<n: h=Wx,\qquad \hat x=\operatorname{ReLU}(W^T h+b),\qquad \mathcal L=\mathbb E_x\sum_i I_i(x_i-\hat x_i)^2. Here I_i are feature-importance weights. These are synthetic features supplied by the experimenter.

  1. Writing w_i for column i of W, expand the preactivation of reconstructed feature i. Identify the self-term and interference. Why does sparsity help without making interference vanish?
  2. Encode each colour–shape pair with a separate one-hot coordinate. Construct a perfect linear probe for “red”. Does this prove that a dedicated red coordinate is used by the model’s computation?
Solution
  1. \hat x_i=\operatorname{ReLU}(\|w_i\|^2x_i+\sum_{j\ne i}w_i^Tw_jx_j+b_i). Sparsity reduces typical coactivation; it does not force the cross-inner-products to zero. Bias and ReLU can reject weak interference, at the cost of discarding some signal. A claim about performance also needs the distribution of feature magnitudes and co-occurrences.
  2. Assign probe weight 1 to every red–shape coordinate and 0 elsewhere. It predicts red perfectly on the one-hot inputs, although the code has no separate red coordinate. Decodability alone neither identifies the model’s chosen factorisation nor proves downstream causal use.

Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders, Gao et al., 2024 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

Top-K sparsity, scaling and evaluation; compare reconstruction with the separate tests of feature quality.

Jumping Ahead: Improving Reconstruction Fidelity with JumpReLU Sparse Autoencoders, Rajamanoharan et al., 2024 Importance ●○○○○ Length ●●●○○

Thresholded activations and an L0 objective, trained with straight-through estimators.

Learning Multi-Level Features with Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoders, Bussmann et al., 2025 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

Nested dictionaries as an attempt to capture features at multiple levels of abstraction.

Exercises: what the sparsity penalty buys

A schematic vanilla SAE has z=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_e(x-b_d)+b_e), \hat x=W_dz+b_d, and objective \mathbb E[\|x-\hat x\|_2^2+\lambda\|z\|_1], with decoder-column scale controlled. This schematic is not the objective of every SAE variant.

  1. For a unit decoder direction d and a fixed residual r, minimise \|r-ad\|^2+\lambda a over a\ge0. What bias does L1 introduce?
  2. Without controlling decoder scale, replace d by cd and a by a/c. What happens to reconstruction and the penalty?
  3. Propose a comparison in which better reconstruction would not by itself establish better interpretability.
Solution
  1. Differentiating yields a^*=\max(0,d^Tr-\lambda/2). The active coefficient is shrunk relative to the nonnegative least-squares estimate. The factor of two changes if reconstruction loss includes a factor 1/2.
  2. Reconstruction is unchanged while the penalty decreases by 1/c: an uncontrolled scaling degeneracy.
  3. Compare methods at matched sparsity and dictionary size, then separately measure held-out concept detection, intervention specificity and downstream loss recovery. A dictionary can improve reconstruction by encoding nuisance variation without improving the chosen task. None of these measurements alone certifies a complete ontology.

Designed for interpretability: training networks from scratch

Here interpretability changes the architecture or constraints of the task model during its own training. Evaluate both the resulting explanations and the capability cost. These are different interventions from fitting an explanatory model to an already-trained network.

Weight-sparse transformers have interpretable circuits, Gao et al., 2025; ICML 2026 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

Start with the de novo models: constrain weights during training, then prune circuits for selected tasks. Examine the capability–interpretability tradeoff and the limits of scaling sparse models; the later dense-model bridge experiment is a separate surrogate approach.

Bilinear MLPs enable weight-based mechanistic interpretability, Pearce et al., 2024; ICLR 2025 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Bilinear layers make weight-based analysis tractable in trained image and small language models. Exact quadratic structure helps analysis, but does not by itself assign human meanings to components.

B-cos Networks: Alignment is All We Need for Interpretability, Böhle, Fritz and Schiele, 2022 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

An optional vision example: train with transforms that encourage weight–input alignment and yield input-dependent contribution maps. This explanation target differs from recovering an LLM algorithm.

Exercise: tractable computation versus meaningful components

For a bias-free bilinear layer y=W_o[(W_ax)\odot(W_bx)], let a_j^T,b_j^T be the rows of W_a,W_b. Show that y_k=x^TQ_kx,\qquad Q_k=\frac12\sum_j(W_o)_{kj}(a_jb_j^T+b_ja_j^T). What does this exact representation buy, and what remains to be shown before calling its eigenvectors interpretable features?

Solution

Expand y_k=\sum_j(W_o)_{kj}(a_j^Tx)(b_j^Tx) and use x^Ta_jb_j^Tx=x^Tb_ja_j^Tx. Symmetrising gives the stated Q_k. Its eigendecomposition exposes exact quadratic contributions to this output coordinate. It does not establish semantic meaning, uniqueness within degenerate eigenspaces, or the role of those contributions in the complete network; these require further evidence.

Learned surrogates: transcoders and circuit tracing

Here the original model already exists. We train a more interpretable approximation to some of its computation, then test how well explanations of that approximation transfer to the original. Read the dense-to-sparse bridges experiment in the weight-sparse-transformer paper as another example.

Transcoders Find Interpretable LLM Feature Circuits, Dunefsky, Chlenski and Nanda, 2024 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

A precursor to cross-layer transcoders: fit a wide, sparsely activating replacement to a trained dense MLP’s input–output map, then analyse the replacement’s feature interactions.

Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models, Ameisen et al., 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●●○

Cross-layer transcoders and attribution graphs: read the replacement-model construction and its limitations before interpreting edges as explanations of the original model.

On the Biology of a Large Language Model, Lindsey et al., 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●●○

Choose one case study, inspect its graph and interventions, and separate observations from the proposed computational story. Pair with the Circuit Tracing methods paper.

Exercise: reconstruction is not mechanism equivalence

Construct f,g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R that agree on a training distribution supported on [-1,1] but disagree after an intervention that sets x=2. What additional checks would you want before transferring an attribution graph from a replacement model to the original?

Solution

Take f(x)=x and g(x)=x+\operatorname{ReLU}(x-1). They agree everywhere on the stipulated support, but f(2)=2 and g(2)=3. Check the original and replacement under the actual interventions, relevant held-out distributions, and matched behavioural metrics; inspect reconstruction-error contributions and mechanisms the replacement leaves fixed. Agreement on ordinary inputs is insufficient to guarantee agreement under interventions.

Circuit discovery, causal scrubbing and causal abstraction

Towards Automated Circuit Discovery for Mechanistic Interpretability, Conmy et al., 2023 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

ACDC searches the original model’s computational graph for task-relevant edges using patching. Unlike a transcoder, it does not train a replacement MLP; dataset, metric and corruption scheme define the discovery problem.

Causal Scrubbing: a method for rigorously testing interpretability hypotheses, Chan et al., Redwood Research, 2022 Importance ●●●●● Length ●●●○○

Read sections 2–4 and limitations: test a specified explanation with constrained resampling. Distinguish the original proposal from the later retrospective comments.

Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks, Geiger et al., 2021 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Interchange interventions test whether a neural representation plays the causal role assigned by a higher-level model.

Finding Alignments Between Interpretable Causal Variables and Distributed Neural Representations, Geiger et al., 2023; CLeaR 2024 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

Distributed Alignment Search learns subspaces aligned with hypothesised variables; distinguish fitting the alignment from testing it.

Causal Abstraction for Mechanistic Interpretability, Geiger et al., JMLR 2025 Importance ●○○○○ Length ●●●●●

Longer theoretical reference: maps between high- and low-level interventions, unifying several interpretability methods. Read selected sections.

How to use and interpret activation patching, Heimersheim and Nanda, 2024 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

Practical companion on corruption, noising versus denoising, redundancy and metric choice.

Exercises: interchange and redundancy

For an orthogonal rotation R, coordinate projector P, base state h_b and source state h_s, a subspace interchange is h'=R^T[(I-P)Rh_b+PRh_s]=h_b+Q(h_s-h_b),\qquad Q=R^TPR.

  1. Show that Q is an orthogonal projector and that (I-Q)h'=(I-Q)h_b. What would an evaluation on the same pairs used to learn R fail to establish?
  2. Let clean inputs be A=B=1 and corrupted inputs A=B=0. Compare individually restoring one input and individually corrupting one input for C=A\land B and C=A\lor B.
Solution
  1. Q^T=Q and Q^2=R^TPRR^TPR=Q. Hence (I-Q)Q=0, proving complement preservation. Fitting an alignment to examples does not establish generalisation to held-out interventions, or rule out competing high-level explanations.
  2. For AND, restoring either input alone leaves 0; corrupting either alone destroys the clean output. For OR, restoring either alone gives 1; corrupting either alone leaves 1. The apparent importance of a component depends on intervention direction and redundancy.

Interpreting Language Model Parameters, Bushnaq et al., Goodfire, 2026 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●●●

Neural Networks Are Already Clean, Nick Cammarata, 2026 Importance ●○○○○ Length ●●●●○

Optional contemporary counterpoint: an informal working hypothesis about neuron interpretability, not an established refutation of superposition.

Exercises: geometry and the choice of parts
  1. Let h(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta). Distinguish its manifold dimension, linear-span dimension and the number of dictionary directions one might use to approximate it. Why might more SAE latents fail to imply more underlying concepts?
  2. For f(x)=W_2W_1x and invertible R, show that \widetilde W_1=RW_1, \widetilde W_2=W_2R^{-1} preserves the function. Does deleting hidden coordinate i before and after this change of basis generally preserve the same ablated function?
Solution
  1. The circle is a one-dimensional manifold with a two-dimensional linear span. A dictionary can use arbitrarily many local directions to describe it. Counting those elements counts a chosen discretisation, not necessarily independent semantic factors.
  2. W_2R^{-1}RW_1=W_2W_1. Deleting coordinate i in the new basis instead inserts I-R^{-1}e_ie_i^TR between W_2 and W_1, generally different from I-e_ie_i^T. Exact functional or parameter reconstruction does not uniquely pick the parts whose interventions should be meaningful.

Feature discovery through the empirical NTK

Feature Identification via the Empirical NTK, Jennifer Lin, 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

Eigenspaces of trained-model eNTKs recover known features in Toy Models of Superposition and a modular-addition MLP. Read the kernel variants and the rotation within feature subspaces; this is evidence in small models, not a general feature-identification theorem.

Mean field sequence: an introduction, Dmitry Vaintrob and Lauren Greenspan, 2026 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●○○○

A complementary theory-led approach to interpretable structure, including modular addition. Distinguish adaptive mean-field limits from the frozen NTK limit; computing a trained model’s eNTK does not assume its training was lazy.

A tale of three theories: sparsity, frustration, and statistical field theory, Dmitry Vaintrob, 2026 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●○○○

Optional preliminary research write-up connecting sparse denoising, interference and frustration-induced structure across scales. A bridge from TMoS and computation in superposition to statistical-physics explanations.

Exercise: a kernel subspace is not a unique feature dictionary

For scalar model output f_\theta, at a fixed trained checkpoint define J_{a\mu}=\frac{\partial f_\theta(x_a)}{\partial\theta_\mu},\qquad K=JJ^T. For vector outputs, one can retain sample–output pairs as matrix indices, or analyse a selected output’s kernel. Specify the convention before comparing spectra.

  1. Show that K is positive semidefinite. If Ku=\lambda u, \|u\|=1 and \lambda>0, show that v=J^Tu/\sqrt\lambda is a unit eigenvector of J^TJ.
  2. If a top eigenvalue is repeated, can the spectrum alone select a unique basis of features? What additional evidence would help?
Solution
  1. z^TKz=\|J^Tz\|^2\ge0. Also \|v\|^2=u^TKu/\lambda=1, and J^TJv=\lambda v. Data-space modes and parameter-space modes are related, but live in different spaces.
  2. Any orthogonal rotation inside a degenerate eigenspace is still an eigenbasis. A spectral gap can identify a subspace without identifying its preferred axes. Ground-truth feature alignment in a toy system, an explicitly motivated basis-selection criterion, and held-out causal tests provide different additional evidence. A large eigenvalue alone is not a semantic label.

Activation plateaus: Heimersheim and collaborators

Activation Plateaus: Where and How They Emerge, Matthew Shinkle and Stefan Heimersheim, 2025 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●○○○

Start here: interpolating early activations can yield flat downstream responses separated by sharp transitions. GPT-2 Large experiments trace their gradual sharpening across layers to MLP sensitivity.

[Interim research report] Activation plateaus & sensitive directions in GPT2, Stefan Heimersheim and Jake Mendel, 2024 Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

The original perturbation experiments and error-correction motivation. Read the September 2024 correction: confidence in the sensitive-direction comparison was reduced after identifying baseline effects.

Evidence for feature-specific error correction in LLMs, Francisco Ferreira da Silva and Stefan Heimersheim, 2026 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

Optional follow-up comparing contrastive candidate feature directions with mixtures of directions, connecting plateau geometry to the error-correction hypothesis.

Exercise: a plateau along which direction?

Let F be the remainder of a network after a chosen activation layer. Along a straight interpolation h(t)=(1-t)h_A+th_B, define y(t)=F(h(t)). The chain rule gives \frac{dy}{dt}=J_F(h(t))(h_B-h_A).

  1. Does a small derivative along this path imply a small Jacobian in every direction? Give a counterexample.
  2. For residual blocks h_{\ell+1}=h_\ell+g_\ell(h_\ell), derive the recurrence for v_\ell=dh_\ell/dt. Why can sensitivity sharpen across layers, and why is multiplying the norms of the MLP Jacobians not the exact calculation?
  3. What comparison would help distinguish a learned plateau around real activations from an artefact of the chosen perturbation distribution?
Solution
  1. No. For F(x_1,x_2)=100x_2, a path parallel to the first coordinate has zero derivative although the Jacobian norm is 100. Flatness along one path need not imply a robust neighbourhood in all directions.
  2. v_{\ell+1}=[I+J_{g_\ell}(h_\ell)]v_\ell. Ordered Jacobian products can amplify some path segments more than others; sharpening is possible, not guaranteed. Residual identities and the alignment of vectors and singular directions matter. Products of operator norms give bounds, not generally equalities.
  3. Compare real and matched synthetic base activations with the same perturbation procedure; control scale, covariance, layer, path and output metric. Examine several directions and both norm-preserving and straight paths. Even a robust difference does not by itself identify discrete features or establish error correction as the unique explanation.

Computation in superposition

Mathematical Models of Computation in Superposition, Hänni, Mendel, Vaintrob and Chan, 2024 Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●●○

Constructive results for sparse computation in narrower networks; distinguish existence from what SGD learns in language models.

Compressed Computation is (probably) not Computation in Superposition, Bhagat et al., including Heimersheim, 2026 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

A particular toy benchmark’s apparent advantage is explained by a label-mixing term. This is a benchmark critique, not an impossibility theorem.

Compressed Computation under L⁴ Loss is likely Computation in Superposition, Ferreira da Silva and Heimersheim, 2026 Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

Read beside the preceding negative result: a changed loss elicits behaviour explained using sparse codewords and pseudoinverse decoding.

Exercise: changing which errors matter

Compare residual vectors (2,0,0,0) and (1,1,1,1) under \sum_i e_i^2 and \sum_i e_i^4. Compute both gradients. Why is a change in training outcome under the new objective insufficient on its own to establish computation in superposition?

Solution

Both squared losses are 4; the fourth-power losses are 16 and 4. The coordinate gradients are 2e_i and 4e_i^3. Fourth-power loss penalises concentrated large errors more strongly. That motivates a different solution but does not identify its mechanism: inspect the learned representation, decoding and intervention evidence.
