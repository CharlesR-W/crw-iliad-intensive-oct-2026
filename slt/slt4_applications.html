4.1 Stagewise development and phase transitions

What this is: Notebook 2 §2.3 said the posterior changes basin at critical sample sizes. These papers track \hat\lambda along a training run and use its plateaus and jumps to divide training into stages.

Dynamical versus Bayesian Phase Transitions in a Toy Model of Superposition, Chen, Lau, Mendel, Wei & Murfet (2023) Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

In a small autoencoder whose critical points are regular polygons, the learning coefficients of those polygons predict the posterior’s phase transitions in n, and the same critical points organize what SGD training does.

Loss Landscape Degeneracy and Stagewise Development in Transformers, Hoogland, Wang, Farrugia-Roberts, Carroll, Wei & Murfet (TMLR 2025) Importance ●●●●○ Length ●●●○○

\hat\lambda measured through training of a small language model and an in-context regression transformer, with its changes marking stages that line up with changes in what the model computes.

Differentiation and Specialization of Attention Heads via the Refined Local Learning Coefficient, Wang, Hoogland, van Wingerden, Furman & Murfet (ICLR 2025) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

The estimator restricted to a subset of the weights or of the data (the refined LLC), used to follow individual attention heads as they take on separate roles during training. Also on arXiv as 2410.02984.

4.2 Susceptibilities

What this is: in Notebook 1’s dictionary, a derivative of a posterior expectation with respect to a small change of the “Hamiltonian” is a susceptibility, and it equals a posterior covariance. Here the change is a reweighting of the data distribution.

Structural Inference: Interpreting Small Language Models with Susceptibilities, Baker, Wang, Hoogland & Murfet (2025) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Shifting the data distribution slightly and measuring the first-order response of a local posterior expectation, estimated with SGLD samples, gives per-component and per-token attribution scores whose low-rank structure separates functional modules of a small transformer.

Towards Spectroscopy: Susceptibility Clusters in Language Models, Gordon, Baker, Wang, Snell, van Wingerden & Murfet (2026) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Susceptibilities to upweighting a token in a context, computed as covariances under the localized tempered posterior, cluster tokens that are predicted for similar reasons; applied to a 14M-parameter model.

4.3 Bayesian influence functions

What this is: classical influence functions ask how a trained parameter moves when one training point is reweighted, and need an inverse Hessian; the Bayesian version asks how a posterior expectation moves, which is again a covariance under the local posterior.

Bayesian Influence Functions for Hessian-Free Data Attribution, Kreer, Wu, Adam, Furman & Hoogland (2025) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Replaces the inverse-Hessian step of the classical influence function by a covariance of per-sample losses under the local tempered posterior, estimated by SGLD, so that it is defined when the Hessian is singular and scales to large models.

The Loss Kernel: A Geometric Probe for Deep Learning Interpretability, Adam, Furman & Hoogland (2025) Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●●○○

The covariance matrix of per-sample losses under low-loss-preserving perturbations of the parameters, used as a similarity between data points and compared with semantic hierarchies on ImageNet.

4.4 Minimum description length and compressibility

What this is: Notebook 1 §1.3 read the free energy as a code length; \lambda is then the coefficient of \log n in the description length of the model, and the question is whether it predicts how well a trained network compresses.

Compressibility Measures Complexity: Minimum Description Length Meets Singular Learning Theory, Urdshals, Lau, Hoogland, van Wingerden & Murfet (2025) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

Extends the minimum description length principle to models with degenerate minima and reports, across the Pythia models and several compression schemes, that \hat\lambda tracks (sometimes linearly) how compressible a network is.

4.5 Programs and singularities

What this is: a line of work relating the structure of a Turing machine to the local geometry of the loss of a statistical model that encodes it. Listed for completeness; it is not on the day’s path and is not assessed here.

Geometry of Program Synthesis, Clift, Murfet & Wallbridge (2021) Importance ●○○○○ Length ●●●●○

Synthesis of Turing machines set up as Bayesian inference over a smooth parameter space, so that programs appear as singularities of the loss, with small experiments.

Programs as Singularities, Murfet & Troiani (2025) Importance ●○○○○ Length ●●●●○

A correspondence between the structure of a Turing machine and the Taylor expansion of a negative log-likelihood at the critical point that encodes it.

4.6 Background for the whole day

Readings that serve any of Notebook 1–Notebook 3.

Keeping neural networks simple by minimizing the description length of the weights, Hinton & van Camp (COLT 1993) Importance ●●○○○ Length ●●○○○

The code-length reading of the posterior volume (Notebook 1 §1.3), from the minimum-description-length side.

Recent Advances in Algebraic Geometry and Bayesian Statistics, Watanabe (2022) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●●○

The author’s survey of the theory behind Notebook 2: the free energy expansion, the poles of \zeta, and the generalization error.

A Widely Applicable Bayesian Information Criterion, Watanabe (JMLR 2013) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

The source of Notebook 3’s identity \mathbb E_{1/\log n}[nL_n]\approx F_n.

Deep Learning is Singular, and That’s Good, Wei, Murfet, Gong, Li, Gell-Redman & Quella (2020) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

The case that neural networks are not regular models and that this is the right starting point for a theory of them.

Singular learning theory: exercises, Furman (LessWrong, 2024) Importance ●●●○○ Length ●●●○○

A problem set on the volume computations of Notebook 2, with the same toy losses.

For reference (books): Algebraic Geometry and Statistical Learning Theory, Watanabe (Cambridge University Press, 2009), the theory of Notebook 2 with proofs; Mathematical Theory of Bayesian Statistics, Watanabe (CRC Press, 2018), the regular case of Notebook 1 and the general case, with the WBIC.
