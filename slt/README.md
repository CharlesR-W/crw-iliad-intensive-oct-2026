# Singular Learning Theory: notebooks

Four notebooks built to `SLT_SPECS_v1.md` (CRW's 2026-10-09 direction), then streamlined on 2026-10-14: the day is Bayesian statistics in thermodynamic language, plus the learning coefficient $\lambda$. The notebooks are a backup to the LessWrong introductions (Carroll's DSLT sequence, Hoogland's posts), which each page links first; they carry no reading lists of their own apart from the applications catalogue in Notebook 4. Singularities get the minimum mention and nothing is organised around degeneracy. Each notebook opens with its question and a keystone reading, every section restates its notation, and the derivations are exercises with worked solutions and small CPU code cells. Nobody is expected to finish everything.

| Notebook | Topic | Page | Notebook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bayesian inference as statistical mechanics.** The dictionary (posterior as Boltzmann distribution, evidence as partition function, free energy, the temperature knob $\beta$) and where it is only formal; posterior concentration (the central limit theorem); Laplace's method and the BIC, the Occam factor as a volume ratio; the Bayes generalization error and the MLE-vs-Bayes comparison, with one misspecified example | [slt1_bayes_statmech.html](slt1_bayes_statmech.html) | [.ipynb](slt1_bayes_statmech.ipynb) |
| 2 | **Free energy and basin volume.** Volume scaling $V(t)\sim ct^\lambda(-\log t)^{m-1}$ and $\bar F_n\approx\lambda\log n-(m-1)\log\log n$; toy losses; $\lambda\le d/2$; the Mellin transform $\zeta(z)=\int K^z\varphi$ and its poles; several basins, the critical sample size, a one-paragraph thermodynamic reading | [slt2_free_energy_volume.html](slt2_free_energy_volume.html) | [.ipynb](slt2_free_energy_volume.ipynb) |
| 3 | **Effective exponents and temperature.** $K=w^4+\delta w^2$ and the discontinuity of $\lambda$; the effective exponent $\lambda_{\rm eff}(n)$ and its crossover at $n\sim\delta^{-2}$; the WBIC, the local learning coefficient estimator, SGLD, hyperparameters read as scales; code on the 1-D model swept over $\beta$, normal crossings, and a two-layer linear network with known $\lambda$ | [slt3_effective_temperature.html](slt3_effective_temperature.html) | [.ipynb](slt3_effective_temperature.ipynb) |
| 4 | **Applications.** A one-line-per-paper catalogue: stagewise development, susceptibilities, Bayesian influence functions, compressibility, programs and singularities (neutral) | [slt4_applications.html](slt4_applications.html) | [.ipynb](slt4_applications.ipynb) |

The pages need a network connection on first load (KaTeX from a CDN). The notebooks run on a CPU in under a minute each and need only numpy, scipy and matplotlib.

## For maintainers

- Never hand-edit the `.html` or `.ipynb` files. Each pair is generated by one builder, `build_<name>.py`, from cells defined with the helpers in `nbkit_slt.py` (`markdown()`, `answer()`, `code()`, `code_answer(stub, solution)`, `image(path, caption)`; the kit's `reading()`/`references()` helpers are unused since the 2026-10-14 streamlining).
- `nbkit_slt.py` and `htmlkit_slt.py` are copies of the Training Dynamics kit (`../td/nbkit_td.py`, `../td/htmlkit_td.py`, taken 2026-10-12) with the sibling-link prefix changed from `td` to `slt`. Do not edit the TD or MDL kits from here; each day is built independently. The flags are the same: `python build_<name>.py` builds (executing the code only when it changed since the cached outputs in `_exec_cache/`, which is git-ignored); `--check` reports staleness without executing; `--rerun` forces execution; `--run` executes and times every code cell.
- `checks/slt1_checks.py`, `slt2_checks.py`, `slt3_checks.py` verify the closed forms used in the solutions numerically (`python checks/sltN_checks.py` from this directory). The 2026-10-14 presentation pass added `checks/<stem>_*.py`, which verify the numbers in the new figures and captions and the solution corrections made then.
- **Illustrations.** Each notebook carries conceptual figures (SVG/PNG in `assets/figures/`), embedded with `nbkit_slt.image(path, caption)`; they are drawn by `assets/figures/make_<stem>_figures.py` (numpy and matplotlib, a few seconds each). Files named `<stem>_lw_*.png` are reproduced from the LessWrong posts credited in their captions. Rerun the figure script after editing it, then rebuild the notebook.
- Review logs and briefs for the 2026-10-14 presentation and streamlining passes are in `../reviews/slt/2026-10-14-*.md`.
- `_local_prior_2026-10-12/` holds an earlier, unfinished parallel draft with different stems. It is superseded and unlinked; kept for salvage only.
- The SGLD code in Notebook 3 is the update rule of Lau et al. (2023) as implemented in Furman's `intro_lc.ipynb` (in `_sources/nb/`), rewritten in numpy; the examples follow devinterp's `normal_crossing.ipynb` and the two-layer linear network of the older worksheet, at a size that runs on a laptop. Estimates for losses with multiplicity $m=2$ come out low by the $1/\log(n\beta)$ correction, as the page says.
- CRW's effective-theory argument is a separate write-up, not a section of these notebooks.
- Sources on disk: `_sources/` (the older worksheet with its LaTeX and PDF, Furman's slides, the devinterp notebooks). Exercises adapted from the worksheet: 4.1–4.2 (two basins, Notebook 2 §2.3), the regular-case volume, the cubic reparametrization, and the $\lambda\le d/2$ bound (Notebook 2 §2.1), the two-layer linear network coefficients (Notebook 3 §3.2).
