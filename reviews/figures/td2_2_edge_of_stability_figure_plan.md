### F2 — Exercise 2.1: "no real flow passes through these points" · **P3**
- **Anchor:** Exercise 2.1 solution, part 3, after "The gradient-descent map $x\mapsto(1-\eta\lambda)x$ is decreasing when $\eta\lambda>1$." It could also go in the exercise statement, as a hint image.
- **What it shows:** three small panels of iterates $x_k$ against $t=k\eta$ on $L=\tfrac12\lambda x^2$. (i) $\eta\lambda=0.5$: positive, decaying dots with the exact flow $e^{at}$ drawn through them. - **Caption:** *Gradient descent on $\tfrac12\lambda x^2$. For $\eta\lambda<1$ a smooth flow passes through the iterates. For $\eta\lambda>1$ they alternate in sign, and no solution of a real one-dimensional ODE can pass through them.*

### F4 — §2.3 opening: weight-space cartoon of GD, central flow and gradient flow · **P2**
- **Anchor:** §2.3, after "**The idea.** At EoS the iterate is $w_k=w+\delta_k$: a slowly moving centre plus a fast oscillation."
- **What it shows:** a schematic in a 2-D slice of a curved valley (contours). It has three curves:
  - GD (blue) zigzags between the valley walls.
  - The central flow (black) is a smooth curve down the middle of the zigzag.
  - Gradient flow (red) leaves along a different path into the sharper region.

  Add an inset or an arrow labelled "$\delta_k$, oscillation along $u$", and a dashed curve labelled "$S=2/\eta$".
- **Why here:** the central flow is about the *time-averaged* trajectory, and "average out the oscillation" needs a picture of what is averaged. It also shows at once why the central flow differs from gradient flow. This is the canonical cartoon of the central-flows paper.
- **Source:** (c), a schematic we draw ourselves in matplotlib with synthetic curves. It is modelled on Cohen, Damian, Talwalkar, Kolter & Lee 2025 (arXiv 2410.24206) **Fig. 2** (verified: "In this illustrative cartoon of the weight-space dynamics, gradient descent (blue) takes an oscillatory path… central flow (black)… gradient flow (red) takes a different path"). That paper is under the arXiv non-exclusive licence, so redraw it and credit "after Cohen et al. 2025, Fig. 2". Their Fig. 7 has the same cartoon next to real loss and sharpness curves, which could be a pointer in the readings.
- **Caption:** *Cartoon after Cohen et al. 2025, Fig. 2. At the edge of stability gradient descent bounces between the walls of a valley. The central flow follows the time-averaged path, sliding along the surface $S=2/\eta$, while gradient flow, which knows nothing about $\eta$, heads into the sharper region.*

### F6 — §2.4: the rod · **P3**
- **Anchor:** §2.4, after "At EoS the iterates jump back and forth, but the rod moves smoothly: its midpoint drifts and its length changes slowly."

- **Caption:** *Consecutive gradient-descent iterates (dots) joined into rods. The iterates jump across the valley every step, but the rod's midpoint moves smoothly, and its length grows and shrinks with the oscillation. Rod Flow tracks the midpoint and the rod's length.*

### F11 — §2.8 WSD: river valley and the WSD loss curve · **P2**
- **Anchor:** §2.8, after "**WSD and the river valley.** [Wen et al.] picture the pretraining loss as a *river valley*: a flat river direction with steep hillsides."
- **What it shows:** two panels.
  - **Left:** contours of $\tfrac12\gamma h^2+\ell(r)$, with three noisy trajectories: constant $\eta_{\max}$ (wide bouncing, far along the river), constant $\eta_{\min}$ (tight, short) and WSD (wide, then collapsing onto the river at the end).
  - **Right:** *expected* loss against step for the three schedules.

  For the expected loss, use the exact variance recursion $v_{k+1}=(1-\eta_k\gamma)^2v_k+\eta_k^2\sigma^2$, from Exercise 2.8(b)'s hint, so the curves are smooth and need no seed averaging; $E[L]=\tfrac12\gamma v_k+\ell(r_k)$. The WSD curve tracks the constant-$\eta_{\max}$ curve, then drops below both at the decay.

  I prototyped this with $\gamma=\sigma=1$, $\eta_{\max}=0.4$, $\eta_{\min}=0.02$, 3000 steps, decay over the last 15%, and a bounded river $\ell(r)=1/(1+r/R)$. The final losses were 0.57 for constant max, 0.86 for constant min and 0.45 for WSD.
- **Why here:** the "loss drops sharply when you decay" curve is the canonical WSD image, and "bouncing hides progress" is a spatial claim. The exercise derives the formula but nothing shows it. **Note:** the exercise uses $\ell=-r$, which is unbounded below. The figure needs a bounded river, and its caption should say it uses one.
- **Source:** (b), generated by us, as a static PNG in the default text. **Alternative:** Wen et al. 2025 (arXiv 2410.05192, **CC BY 4.0**, so embeddable) **Fig. 1**, "The Distinctive Loss Curve produced by WSD", a real pretraining curve, and **Fig. 2(a)**, the river-valley cartoon with large, small and decayed learning-rate trajectories. If CRW prefers a real-model curve as the hook, embed their Fig. 1 and keep only the left panel of ours.
- **Caption:** *A river valley: steep in $h$, gently descending in $r$, with noise across the valley. A large step makes fast progress along the river, but its bouncing inflates the loss. A small step bounces little but crawls. Warmup–stable–decay takes the large step for most of the run, then decays, removing the bounce and revealing the progress made.*
