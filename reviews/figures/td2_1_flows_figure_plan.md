## Proposals, in notebook order

### F1 · "The iterates lie on a flow, just not gradient flow" (P1)

1. **Anchor:** §1.2 "Backward error analysis", right after the first paragraph, which ends "…is a qualitative statement about what the discretization does." It comes before "The method is standard in numerical analysis". (Alternative: just before "**The first correction for GD.**" CRW's call. The early position makes it the hook.)
2. **What it shows:** GD on a 2-D quadratic L = ½(λ₁c₁² + λ₂c₂²) with λ = (1, 8), start c = (3, 1), so the sharp coordinate is c₂. Three panels share that setup, with η chosen so that ηS = ηλ₂ = **0.4, 0.9, 1.6**. Each panel has light loss contours, GD iterates as black dots, the GF path (solid blue), the IGR path c_i(0)e^{−λ_i(1+ηλ_i/2)t} (dashed orange), and, in (a) and (b), the **exact modified flow** c_i(0)(1−ηλ_i)^{t/η} (thin black). That curve passes exactly through every iterate.
   - (a) ηS = 0.4: the IGR curve nearly coincides with the exact modified flow; GF is visibly off.
   - (b) ηS = 0.9: the dots still lie on a smooth (exact modified) flow, but the IGR curve is now well off it.
   - (c) ηS = 1.6: the iterates zigzag across the valley (sign flip of c₂ each step), with dots joined by a thin line. No exact flow is drawn. Annotation: "no real gradient flow passes through these points (Ex. 1.2.c)."
   - An optional second row plots the sharp coordinate c₂ against t = kη. My prototype showed it makes the "faster than the flow" point of §1.1 immediately readable (GD dots fall below the GF curve).
3. **Why here:** the forward/backward distinction is the conceptual move of the section, and it is currently prose-only. A student sees at once that the GD dots sit on a smooth curve that is *not* the gradient flow, which is what "modified equation" means. Panels (b) and (c) preview the two failure modes of §1.3: the first correction becomes insufficient, and then no real gradient flow exists at all. Panel (c) also shows §1.1's oscillating regime, so §1.1 needs no figure of its own.
4. **Source:** (b) generate ourselves, as a pre-rendered static PNG `assets/figures/td2_1_bea_quadratic.png`. Everything is closed form (no ODE solve); it takes well under 1 s. η = 0.05, 0.1125, 0.2. Plot t ∈ [0, ~1.5] for the time-series row. (HLW ch. IX has comparable pictures, but it is paywalled Springer material and in an ODE rather than an optimization setting. Self-generating is cleaner.)
5. **Priority:** P1.
6. **Caption draft:** *Gradient descent on a quadratic valley, with sharpness S = 8. (a, b) The iterates (dots) lie exactly on the trajectory of a modified flow (thin black), not on gradient flow (blue). The first-order modified flow, gradient flow on L + (η/4)‖∇L‖² (dashed), matches it well at ηS = 0.4 and poorly at ηS = 0.9. (c) Once ηS > 1 the sharp coordinate flips sign every step, and no real gradient flow can pass through the iterates.*

### F2 · IGR on the ab model: GD crosses the gradient-flow conservation curves toward flatter minima (P1)

1. **Anchor:** §1.2, at the end of the IGR paragraph after "…the code after Exercise 1.2.d measures both." It comes immediately before "#### Exercise 1.2.a — The recipe". (Alternative: at the head of Exercise 1.2.d, after "Write $r=ab-1$ and $D=a^2-b^2$ (the *imbalance*)." Putting it in the default text means students who skip the exercise still see it.) The opening pitch could add "(pictured in §1.2)" after "implicit gradient regularization".
2. **What it shows:** two panels, using the notebook's own start (a, b) = (2, 0.1) and step sizes η = 0.05, 0.2, 0.35, so the figure agrees with the printed numbers of the 1.2.d code cell (81% / 42% / 16%).
   - **(a) Phase plane** (a, b) ∈ [−0.2, 2.6]². Faint grey level sets of a²−b² (the curves gradient flow is confined to, since D is conserved). The minimum hyperbola ab = 1 is coloured by the sharpness of each minimum, √(D²+4), with a colourbar, and the balanced point (1, 1) is marked "+". The GF path (black) runs along its D-curve. GD paths (dots, one colour per η) cross D-curves toward balance, with the IGR flow for each η dashed in the same colour. The shift is only about 0.1 in a, so a **zoom inset** on the endpoints ([1.93, 2.08] × [0.46, 0.53]) is essential. My prototype confirmed the inset makes it legible.
   - **(b) Sharpness of the minimum reached against η** (η from 0.01 to 0.45): GD (dots), the IGR flow (line), and the GF value (dotted horizontal at 4.46). Add a top axis in ηS₀ (S₀ ≈ 4.1). Prototype values: at η = 0.05, GD 4.448 vs IGR 4.451; at η = 0.25, 4.32 vs 4.42; at η = 0.45, 3.45 vs 4.40.
3. **Why here:** this is the canonical IGR picture (Barrett & Dherin's Fig. 1a is the same model). It makes three things visible that the text states abstractly. (i) GF conserves imbalance and GD does not. (ii) Less imbalance means a flatter minimum. (iii) Panel (b) is the most direct visual of the section's sobering message: the first-order correction gets the direction right at every η, and the size right only at small ηS. GD's flattening grows much faster than IGR predicts once ηS ≳ 1.
4. **Source:** (b) generate ourselves; static PNG `assets/figures/td2_1_igr_ab.png`. Recipe: reuse `grad_ab`, `hess_ab`, `rk4` and `gd` from the notebook. Run to T = 40 (8000 RK4 steps for the flows); panel (b) uses 23 values of η with 4000 RK4 steps each, about 5 s in total. *Paper alternative, not recommended:* Barrett & Dherin (arXiv 2009.11162), Fig. 1a, verified. It is a phase-space plot of E = (y − abx)²/2 with gradient-flow arrows, the minimum line, and GD trajectories from (2.8, 3.5) and from (75, 74.925) (off plot) at a small and a moderate learning rate, together with the modified flow. But it also overlays an *explicit*-regularization path with a very large μ, which is a confound for this section's point. Its far-off start is needed to make the effect large, its step sizes aren't the notebook's, and its licence is arXiv non-exclusive (permission needed to embed). Cite it in the caption as "cf. Barrett & Dherin Fig. 1a".
5. **Priority:** P1.
6. **Caption draft:** *L(a, b) = ½(ab − 1)², started from (2, 0.1). Gradient flow (black) conserves the imbalance a² − b² and slides along one of the grey curves to the minimum hyperbola, coloured by sharpness. Gradient descent (dots) crosses those curves toward the balanced, flatter minima, and the first-order IGR flow (dashed) predicts the direction of the shift (inset). (b) The IGR flow predicts the size of the flattening well only while ηS₀ is small; at larger steps GD flattens far more. Cf. Barrett & Dherin (2021), Fig. 1a.*

### F3 · The ηS number line: what is guaranteed where (P2)

1. **Anchor:** §1.3 "Is the expansion valid?", directly under the "Short version" paragraph, which ends "…for those who want to see an asymptotic series fail concretely." Alternatively at the head of "**Where training sits.**"
2. **What it shows:** a schematic with one horizontal axis, ηS from 0 to about 4.5, and coloured bands with short labels:
   - 0 to about 0.3: "first-order modified flow (IGR) accurate";
   - up to 1: "first correction degrades smoothly; series radius on a quadratic";
   - 1–2: "GD still converges, but no real gradient flow reproduces it (sign flips)";
   - above 2: "minimum unstable for GD".

   Tick marks above the axis:
   - "optimal constant step on an ill-conditioned quadratic: ηS = 2κ/(κ+1) ∈ [1, 2)";
   - "full-batch training: sharpness rises to ≈2/η (edge of stability, Cohen et al.)", an arrow at 2;
   - "heavy ball: instability at 2(1+β)", e.g. 3.8 at β = 0.9, with a note that heavy ball's own expansion needs ηλ ≪ (1−√β)², about 0.003 at β = 0.9.
3. **Why here:** §1.3 makes four separate threshold claims, scattered over about 30 lines and three exercises. One axis shows that the regime where the theory is good and the regime where training actually runs barely overlap, which is the takeaway of the section. It is also a compact bridge to td2_2.
4. **Source:** (c) schematic. It is easiest to draw in matplotlib (`axhspan`/`annotate` on a single axis) in the same figure script; static PNG `assets/figures/td2_1_etaS_regimes.png`. Keep the labels to the claims the notebook makes and cites; don't add new ones.
5. **Priority:** P2.
6. **Caption draft:** *Where the step-size expansion holds, in units of ηS (step size times sharpness). The first-order modified flow is accurate only for ηS well below 1. Between 1 and 2 GD still converges but no real gradient flow reproduces it, and above 2 the minimum repels GD. Full-batch training typically drives ηS to about 2.*

### F5 · Heavy ball's slow manifold in the (θ, v) plane (P2)

1. **Anchor:** §1.5, in the "**The slow manifold.**" paragraph, after "…and both become complex with modulus $\sqrt\beta$. So the expansion is not uniform as $\beta\to1$. At $\beta=0.9$ it needs $\eta\lambda\ll0.003$." It comes before "**The modified flow (Ghosh et al.).**"
2. **What it shows:** heavy ball on the 1-D loss L = log cosh θ (g = tanh θ, curvature ≤ 1) at β = 0.5, where (1−√β)² ≈ 0.086. The axes are θ and v/η. Seven starts, from (±2.4, 0), (±2.4, ±several), and (±1, ±3), show the iterates, with the first 8 iterates as black dots. The leading-order manifold v/η = −g(θ)/(1−β) is a thick grey curve.
   - **(a) η = 0.01** (ηλ_max ≪ 0.086): every start drops almost vertically onto the grey curve in about 5 steps (the β^k transient) and then slides slowly along it. From-rest starts (v = 0) visibly begin *off* the manifold.
   - **(b) η = 0.3** (ηλ_max above 0.086 near θ = 0): the trajectories spiral into the minimum with no slow manifold to follow.
3. **Why here:** "slow manifold", "slaved velocity" and "started from rest vs started on the manifold" are the three ideas that make §1.5's modified flow and its O(η) vs O(η²) caveat make sense, and all three are pure geometry. The picture shows the time-scale separation and its failure condition directly. Goh's Distill article, the core reading, covers the quadratic and the tuning but not this.
4. **Source:** (b) generate ourselves. It can be a static PNG `assets/figures/td2_1_hb_slow_manifold.png`, or, since it costs under 1 s, a new small `code()` cell right after the paragraph; I'd lean static so it's visible without running. Recipe: N = 1500 steps for (a) and 60 for (b). The prototype reads cleanly.
5. **Priority:** P2.
6. **Caption draft:** *Heavy ball (β = 0.5) on L = log cosh θ, plotted as position θ against velocity per step v/η. (a) With a small step, every start collapses within a few steps onto the slow manifold v ≈ −η∇L/(1−β) (grey), then drifts along it; on the manifold, heavy ball is a one-step map of θ alone, so §1.2's recipe applies. Starting from rest begins off the manifold. (b) When ηλ is not small compared with (1−√β)², the two time scales merge and the iterates spiral.*
